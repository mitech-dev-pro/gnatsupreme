# Product

## Register

product

## Users

GNAT Supreme Care serves national, regional, and district administrators, operational staff, school staff, and enrolled members. Administrative users work with high-volume member records, imports, approvals, transfers, claims, reports, configuration, and audit workflows. Members use a simpler self-service experience to review their profile and notifications.

Every role should encounter the same interaction language, while navigation, information density, permissions, and available actions adapt to that role's responsibilities.

## Product Purpose

GNAT Supreme Care provides one dependable place to administer membership and benefit-related operations. It should help users complete sensitive, multi-step work accurately, understand the current state of every record, recover confidently from errors, and identify the next required action without specialist training.

Success means routine work is fast, consequential actions are deliberate, system status is always legible, and users can trust that their changes were recorded correctly.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, clear, efficient.

The experience should create calm confidence during high-volume administrative work. Its voice is direct, respectful, and helpful. It communicates consequences plainly without sounding alarming, bureaucratic, or overly technical.

## Anti-references

- Flashy or stereotypical healthcare styling that uses decoration to imply trust.
- Dense government portals with weak hierarchy, crowded navigation, and long undifferentiated forms.
- Generic dashboards made from repetitive, interchangeable cards.
- Decorative animation, novelty transitions, glass effects, and unexplained icons.
- Role-specific jargon without contextual explanation.
- Interfaces that rely on color alone to communicate status or urgency.
- Destructive actions hidden behind ambiguous controls or browser-native dialogs.

## Design Principles

1. Make the next action obvious. Every workflow should communicate its current state, available action, and likely outcome.
2. Earn trust through clarity. Use explicit labels, readable status language, visible system feedback, and confirmation proportional to risk.
3. Keep one system across every role. Adapt density and permissions without changing the underlying component and interaction vocabulary.
4. Design for real operational pressure. Support scanning, keyboard use, interruption, recovery, and large datasets without unnecessary ceremony.
5. Reveal complexity when it becomes relevant. Keep routine paths concise while making detailed records, explanations, and controls easy to reach.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA across staff and member experiences.

- All workflows must be operable with a keyboard and expose meaningful names, roles, states, and relationships to assistive technology.
- Focus must remain visible and move predictably through navigation, dialogs, validation, and asynchronous updates.
- Status and urgency must never depend on color alone.
- Motion must respect reduced-motion preferences and remain purposeful.
- Layout and content must remain usable at 200% browser zoom and across supported mobile, tablet, and desktop widths.
- Forms must provide persistent labels, useful instructions, and field-level errors that explain how to recover.
- Language should be plain, culturally respectful, and understandable without internal system knowledge.
